48 MHz vs 12 MHz MLAT timestamps

Does the ADSBee 1090's 48 MHz Mode S timestamp give better multilateration (MLAT) than the 12 MHz count the Beast protocol carries, and does it let a smaller array reach a given accuracy? Firmware reading, error budget, Cramér-Rao bounds and a Monte Carlo run of the full chain (traffic, receivers, clock sync from ADS-B aircraft, solver).

Verdict
18.6 nsper-receiver timing error, 48 MHz (ADSBee 1090, after clock sync)
29.8 nsthe same receivers at 12 MHz
1.35-1.7×smaller single-fix position error with 48 MHz, ADSBee-only arrays
14-26 %shorter array for the same accuracy at 50-200 km (median 22 %)
  • 48 MHz helps when every receiver in the solution is an ADSBee 1090. At 12 MHz the counter step (83.3 ns, 24 ns RMS) is the largest error term. At 48 MHz the demodulator's own 14.5 ns jitter dominates and the counter adds only 6 ns.
  • Accuracy: 1.6× better at 100-200 km and 1.35× better at 50 km, where the altitude measurement already carries much of the fix. Monte Carlo and the bound agree.
  • Smaller arrays: far-field error grows with σ·R²/B², so the aperture B needed for a given accuracy shrinks by about √1.6 (14-26 % in the table below). Example, 4 receivers with a 10 km altitude constraint: 100 m at 100 km needs a 84 km array at 48 MHz vs 103 km at 12 MHz. At equal aperture, 3 receivers at 48 MHz do about as well as 4 at 12 MHz (and as 5 at 12 MHz out to about 50 km).
  • Little gain elsewhere: ADSBee m1421 (its 125 ns sync-edge grid dominates: 38 vs 45 ns), and mixed networks with RTL-SDR feeders (their ~55 ns dominates: 549 vs 558 m).
  • Geometry still decides absolute accuracy. Compact arrays (5-20 km) give kilometer-level single fixes at 100-200 km with either clock. 100 m at 100 km needs an array 70-100 km across.
  • Recommendation: keep the standard Beast output at 12 MHz for compatibility. Carry the full 48 MHz count on a separate, explicitly labeled ADSBee MLAT feed (details below).

How the 1090 stamps a frame

  • A 48 MHz count is built from the 24-bit SysTick timer on the 125 MHz system clock (scaled by 48/125, wraps tracked in the SysTick handler): ADSBee::GetMLAT48MHzCounts(), adsbee.cc.
  • The demodulation-begin interrupt latches that count; a PWM slice counting the system clock and a DMA transfer on the sampler's FIFO pull remove the interrupt latency.
  • The pulse sampler reads the comparator at 12 MHz (12 samples per bit). mode_s_soft_demod::DataStart48MHzCounts() averages the mid-bit edges of the first 24 bits (falling edges of 1 bits, rising of 0 bits, so pulse widening with signal level cancels) and returns the data start in 48 MHz counts, finer than one sample.
  • The stamp is the end of data bit 56, 64 µs after the first preamble pulse, for short and long frames (the dump1090 and mlat-client convention, PR #308).
  • Beast output: Write12MHzMLATTimestamp(GetMLAT12MHzCounter()) sends (48 MHz count >> 2) & 0xFFFFFFFFFFFF. The two low bits are dropped. RAW output keeps the full 48 MHz count.

Recommended timestamp format

  • Keep the standard Beast output at 12 MHz. mlat-client, readsb and mlat-server treat a Beast clock as 12 MHz (mlat-server clocksync.py: 12 MHz, 83 ns jitter, 5 ppm maximum frequency error). Sending 48 MHz ticks there would break them silently.
  • ADSBee MLAT feed: send the full 48 MHz count. Same Beast framing with the 6-byte field holding 48 MHz ticks (48 bits wrap after 68 days), on a new ReportingProtocol value appended at the end of the enum, so no existing setting changes meaning. The server learns the rate per receiver (clock_hz on mlat:receivers). The firmware already holds this count, so the timestamp path needs no change.
  • Server weighting: per-frame σ about 19 ns for an ADSBee 1090 at 48 MHz, 30 ns at 12 MHz, 40-45 ns for an m1421, 55-60 ns for an RTL-SDR.
  • Next timing gain, if wanted: the 1090 averages the edges of 24 bits. Averaging all 56 or 112 bits could lower its 14.5 ns jitter by up to 1.5-2.2× if the jitter is independent per edge (not verified; it costs decoder time). That would matter only once the counter is 48 MHz.

Method and limits

  • Simulator: software/adsbee_mlat/sim (package adsbee_mlat_sim), branch t0429. Real Mode S frames with valid parity (DF4/5/11/17/20/21, CPR positions, cross-checked with pyModeS), per-receiver clocks, Beast output. Study scripts in sim/study.
  • The reference server groups frames by their true emission and starts each fix 2 km from the truth, as a tracker would. Its sync fits the offset against each receiver's local arrival time (fitting against emission time leaves a frequency-error × range bias of 5-17 ns).
  • Altitude measurement: true height plus a constant 50 m error per aircraft, in 25 ft steps.
  • Timing terms come from bench data (t-0383 for the 1090, t-0421 for the m1421) and published figures for RTL-SDR. Site multipath and oscillator quality vary in the field; the poor-oscillator and GNSS rows bound their effect.
  • No live-traffic capture in this task: the 1090 on adsbee-ci-pi-0 was in use for another agent's flash backup. No device was flashed, no RF was transmitted, and no feed settings were touched.
